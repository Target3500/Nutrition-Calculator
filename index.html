11. Formula Glossary & Feed Evaluation Tables

Term / ParameterCategory / FormulaClinical & Academic Description
GE (Gross Energy)Energy EvaluationTotal chemical energy liberated upon complete combustion in a bomb calorimeter: (Fat × 9.45) + (Carbs × 4.2) + (Protein × 4.1) Kcal/g.
DE (Digestible Energy)Energy EvaluationApparent absorbed energy after subtracting fecal energy loss from gross energy: DE = GE − FE.
ME (Metabolizable Energy)Energy EvaluationEnergy available for tissue metabolism after subtracting urinary energy (UE) and methane/gas energy (GaE): ME = DE − (UE + GaE).
NE (Net Energy)Energy EvaluationEnergy actually retained for maintenance (NEm) and production (NEp: milk, meat, wool) after heat increment loss: NE = ME − HI.
CD% (Apparent Digestibility)DigestibilityPercentage of ingested nutrient apparently absorbed: [ (Intake − Total Faecal Output) ÷ Intake ] × 100. Overlooks endogenous losses.
True Digestibility (TD %)DigestibilityActual absorbed percentage correcting for metabolic fecal nitrogen / endogenous loss (MFN): [ (Intake − (Faecal Output − MFN)) ÷ Intake ] × 100. Standard MFN: ~0.5g / 100g meal intake.
MFN (Metabolic Fecal Nitrogen)Digestive PhysiologyEndogenous nitrogen in faeces originating from sloughed intestinal epithelial cells, digestive juices, enzymes, and microbial residue rather than unabsorbed feed.
TDN (Total Digestible Nutrients)Energy IndexStandard measure of energy density: TDN % = DCP + DNFE + DCF + (DEE × 2.25). DEE multiplied by 2.25 due to fat's higher caloric density.
CP (Crude Protein)Proximate AnalysisTotal nitrogen determined via Kjeldahl method multiplied by standard factor 6.25: CP % = N % × 6.25. (Assumes 16% average N in protein).
DCP (Digestible Crude Protein)Protein EvaluationThe fraction of crude protein digested and absorbed: DCP % = CP % × Digestion Coefficient of Protein.
NR (Nutritive Ratio)Dietary BalanceRatio of digestible protein to digestible non-protein energy: NR = 1 : [ (TDN − DCP) ÷ DCP ]. Narrow (<1:5) for growth/lactation, Wide (>1:8) for maintenance.
DM (Dry Matter)Moisture LabFeed mass remaining after oven-drying at 100–105°C: DM % = [ (W0 − Wd) ÷ Ws ] × 100. Moisture % = 100 − DM %.
Ash & OM (Organic Matter)Proximate AnalysisAsh is inorganic mineral residue after ignition at 550–600°C: Ash % = [ (Wa − Wc) ÷ Ws ] × 100. Organic Matter: OM % = 100 − Ash %.
CF (Crude Fiber)Fiber ChemistryInsoluble structural carbohydrate residue after successive boiling in weak acid (1.25% H2SO4) and weak alkali (1.25% NaOH): CF % = [ (Dry Residue − Ash) ÷ Ws ] × 100.
EE (Ether Extract / Crude Fat)Fat ChemistryLipids, plant pigments, and fat-soluble vitamins extracted using petroleum ether or diethyl ether in a Soxhlet apparatus. Energy density = 9.45 Kcal/g.
NFE (Nitrogen Free Extract)Soluble CarbsEasily digestible non-structural carbohydrates (starches, simple sugars) determined by subtraction: NFE % = 100 − (Moisture + CP + CF + EE + Ash).
PE (Protein Equivalent)Ruminant NutritionMeasures total nitrogen value for ruminants accounting for true protein and NPN: PE = DTP + (Digestible NPN ÷ 2).
NPN (Non-Protein Nitrogen)Ruminant FeedNitrogen compounds not bound in proteins (e.g., urea, biuret, ammonium salts) converted into microbial crude protein in the rumen.
DTP (Digestible True Protein)Protein QualityDigestible intact amino acid peptide chains excluding non-protein nitrogen fractions.
Corn (Yellow Maize Grain)Feedstuff ProfileHigh-energy cereal grain: DM ~88%, CP ~8.5–9.0%, TDN ~88–90%, CF ~2.2%, EE ~3.8%. Primary source of starch and metabolizable energy.
Soybean Meal (SBM 44/48%)Feedstuff ProfilePremier plant protein concentrate: DM ~89%, CP ~44–48%, TDN ~78–82%, CF ~3.5–6.0%, EE ~1.5%. Balanced essential amino acid profile (rich in lysine).
Wheat BranFeedstuff ProfileMilling byproduct rich in digestible fiber and phosphorus: DM ~89%, CP ~14–16%, TDN ~65–70%, CF ~10–12%. Mild laxative diet balancer.
Alfalfa Hay (Lucerne)Feedstuff ProfileHigh-quality leguminous forage: DM ~90%, CP ~16–20%, TDN ~58–62%, CF ~24–28%. Excellent natural source of calcium, carotene, and structural fiber.
SARA (Subacute Ruminal Acidosis)Clinical PathologyMetabolic disorder occurring when ruminal pH drops below 5.6 for prolonged periods, typically caused by excess rapidly fermentable starch (grain) and insufficient effective fiber (eNDF).
